Why does the Frobenius norm remain invariant under orthogonal transformations?

Reply Fork to my space
Horizon Answered from Lecture 7 · 2 passages 12 found this helpful

Because the Frobenius norm is defined through the trace, and the trace is exactly what an orthogonal matrix leaves alone. For orthogonal Q we have Q⊤Q = I, so [1]

‖QA‖²F = tr((QA)⊤(QA)) = tr(A⊤Q⊤QA) = tr(A⊤A) = ‖A‖²F

Geometrically Q rotates the columns of A without changing their lengths, so nothing is added or lost. Writing A = UΣV⊤ and peeling off both orthogonal factors gives ‖A‖F = ‖Σ‖F — the norm depends only on the singular values, which is the identity Homework 3 Q2(b) asks you to use. [2]
